 **Dr. Anna Birkenbach** and other members of the New England Fishery Management Council (NEFMC)'s Scientific and Statistical Committee (SSC) traveled to the working waterfront town of New Bedford, Massachusetts for their latest meeting. Once one of the world's top whaling ports (visit the excellent [whaling museum](https://www.whalingmuseum.org/)!), New Bedford has survived fishing fleet and port consolidation to remain a top landing location for groundfish, sea scallops, and more, and it is now home to the largest seafood auction on the U.S. East Coast. SSC members got a tour of the [BASE Seafood Auction (Buyers & Sellers Exchange)](https://www.baseseafood.com/index) from plant manager Lenny Pimentel, who patiently answered dozens of questions about prices, supply chains, and auction logistics.

 Those who accepted the smells, crustacean parts, and splatter of fish juices over their now-condemned footwear stayed longer to watch a vessel offloading groundfish, including an Atlantic halibut (pictured below), haddock, and American plaice. Most of the catch is heaped in a bucket by hand and transported by rope, load by load, from the vessel hold below deck to the auction's cleaning and sorting station. Once categorized by size, species, etc., the fish are placed on ice for the following day's auction. SSC members also got to see scallops in boxes from that morning's auction, along with one stray cod (both also pictured below). Nowadays, most buyers participate in the auction online, watching prices tick up or down on computer screens rather than on a chalkboard in a packed room. Much of the seafood that passes through the auction is destined for New York City, where it presumably joins the city's other malodorous ground juices.
